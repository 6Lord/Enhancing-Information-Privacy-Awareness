About PromptSmart

Learn to use AI without giving away more than you mean to.

PromptSmart is an educational resource for employed adults aged 18–35 who use generative AI at work or in daily life. It teaches information privacy through clear explanations, relevant examples and practical steps—not just a list of rules.

Our purpose

Help adults make informed choices when AI is part of work and everyday life.

Generative AI can help draft emails, summarise documents, plan a budget, translate text or explore a personal question. Using it may also involve entering prompts, workplace files or personal details into a service operated by another organisation. The privacy implications depend on the tool, account, settings, information involved and applicable workplace policies.

PromptSmart teaches adults to recognise information, check how a tool handles it, share only what is necessary and review AI-generated content. The lessons support informed choices; they do not replace an employer’s policy, a service’s terms or professional advice.

The essential definitions

Privacy and information privacy

What is privacy?

Privacy is the ability to have boundaries and make informed choices about your personal life, communications and activities without unnecessary intrusion.

It gives people space, dignity and control over who can observe them or make decisions about them.

What is information privacy?

Information privacy is the responsible handling of data that identifies or describes a person. It covers collection, use, sharing, storage, security and deletion.

Examples include a name, ID number, location, photo, salary, employment or client details, health information, messages, device IDs and online behaviour.

Why it matters in our context

AI privacy affects work, relationships and personal choices

An employee might paste a client email, an internal report or a colleague’s details into an AI assistant to save time. In daily life, someone might share a health question, a financial document or a private message. These details can identify people or expose confidential information. Depending on the service and settings, prompts or files may be processed or retained under the provider’s rules. AI outputs can also repeat details, invent claims or make unfair assumptions. Checking first and sharing less helps protect yourself and others.

Work and daily-life examples

A person asks AI to draft a customer reply and includes the customer’s name, order number and complaint details. The writing task may not require those identifiers. At home, uploading a bank statement for budgeting advice may expose account details and transactions that can instead be removed or summarised.

Our principles

Useful, ethical and inclusive

Plain language

Technical ideas are explained without unnecessary jargon, with key terms introduced when they matter.

Respect for people

Privacy is connected to dignity, safety, consent and the right to make informed choices.

Practice over panic

We focus on realistic actions that reduce risk instead of making technology feel frightening.
