Lesson 1 · Understand the information flow

A prompt is information you are sharing

A generative AI assistant uses the prompt and any supplied context to produce an answer. The service has to process what you send. What happens afterwards depends on the provider, the product, your account and its settings. Do not assume a chatbot is private just because the conversation feels personal.

Explore how information moves through an AI tool

1. You enter information

A prompt can contain names, school details, a draft, a photo, a voice recording or details about another person. Uploaded material may reveal more than you intended.

2. The service processes it

The provider’s terms and settings govern handling. Prompts may be logged or retained for service, safety or improvement purposes; check the tool’s current privacy information rather than guessing.

3. AI creates an output

An answer can be inaccurate, biased or unexpectedly repeat details from your prompt. Treat generated text as something to review—not as proof that the information is safe or correct.

Important:

PromptSmart does not know the privacy settings of every AI product. If you cannot confirm that a tool is permitted and appropriate for the information, do not enter personal, sensitive or confidential details. Ask a teacher or trusted adult.

Lesson 2 · A practical decision routine

Use P.A.U.S.E. before you prompt

Take a few seconds to check the information and the tool before asking AI for help.

Open the five-step P.A.U.S.E. guide
P

Personal?

Does the text, file or image identify you or someone else? Look for names, faces, student numbers, contact details, location, marks and sensitive life details.

A

Approved?

Is this specific AI tool allowed by your school or organisation for this task? Check the rules and the provider’s privacy settings and terms.

U

Useful and necessary?

Does the AI need this detail to help? Remove anything that is not needed; use a fictional example or a neutral placeholder instead.

S

Safe to share?

If you are unsure whether the tool can handle the information appropriately, stop and ask a teacher. Never submit passwords, access codes or another person’s private records.

E

Examine the output

Check facts, fairness and privacy before using or sharing the answer. Keep a person responsible for important decisions.

Worked example: ask for help without sharing the record

Worked school example

Ask for help without sharing the record

Risky prompt: “Rewrite this email for me,” followed by a learner’s full name, student number and detailed medical reason for missing class.

Safer prompt: “Help me write a polite email to my teacher asking for an extension because of a personal matter. Keep it brief and respectful.”

The safer version explains the writing task but does not expose identity or health information. Add only details that are genuinely necessary, and use the AI tool only if it is allowed for the task. A learner should review and personalise the draft before sending it.

Guided practice

Apply the lessons to realistic school situations

Choose a topic to open its explanation and challenges. Read each key lesson first, then think through the situations and compare your decisions.

01What can an AI prompt reveal?Recognise direct, sensitive and indirect identifiers in text, files, images and recordings.
Try the challenge
  1. A learner asks AI to edit a draft that includes their full name, student number and teacher’s comments. Which details are unnecessary for editing?
  2. A screenshot has no names but shows the school badge, a unique project and marks. Could these clues identify learners?
  3. A voice note includes a classmate discussing a family problem. Is a transcript request a reason to share it?
  4. A document includes author names, comments and revision history. What should be checked before uploading it?
  5. A prompt describes a learner’s rare circumstances without using their name. Could someone in the school community recognise them?

Key lesson: Names are only one clue. Marks, school context, voice, document details and combined facts can also reveal identity.

02Choose the tool and limit the dataCheck whether the AI service is suitable, then share only what the task requires.
Try the challenge
  1. Your school has not approved a new chatbot. What should you check or ask before using it for an assignment?
  2. A privacy setting says chats are not used to improve the model. Does that by itself explain all data handling?
  3. AI is asked to explain a maths idea. Can a fictional example answer the question without a real learner record?
  4. A prompt contains another learner’s name and story, neither of which is needed. How can you reduce the identifying details?
  5. A service requests access to files or contacts for a simple text question. Is that access necessary for the task?

Key lesson: Check the tool, purpose, terms and school rules. Minimise what you share; one privacy setting is not a complete guarantee.

03Use AI without oversharingProtect confidential work and personal information while getting useful learning help.
Try the challenge
  1. Replace names, student numbers and exact dates with neutral labels before requesting a summary.
  2. Do not paste passwords, access codes or private messages into a chatbot, even if the tool appears helpful.
  3. Ask whether the AI service is approved by your school or organisation before using confidential information.
  4. Use a short fictional example to test a prompt instead of uploading a real disciplinary, medical or family record.
  5. Write a prompt that asks for feedback on an essay without including the learner’s identity or classmates’ comments.

Key lesson: Minimise data, use approved tools and keep human responsibility for the final decision.

04Check AI outputs and respond safelyReview for errors, unfair assumptions and exposed information, and know how to report a mistake.
Try the challenge
  1. An AI summary invents a date in a history assignment. How can you check it before using the summary?
  2. An AI tool labels a learner as dishonest from a short paragraph. Should that judgement guide a decision?
  3. An answer repeats a classmate’s private detail from the prompt. What should you avoid doing with the output?
  4. You pasted a personal detail into a tool by mistake. Which service controls and trusted school contact can you use?
  5. You receive an AI-generated answer that sounds certain but is not supported. Who is responsible for verifying it?

Key lesson: AI can be inaccurate, biased or reveal prompt details. Do not circulate exposed information; report incidents and keep a person accountable.

Review and recall

20 privacy and AI reminders

Use these reminders to revisit the lessons. The quiz focuses on applying them to generative AI and schoolwork situations.

Show all 20 reminders
01

Personal data

Names, contact details and identification numbers can identify a person.

02

Sensitive data

Health, financial, biometric and disciplinary details need extra care.

03

Digital traces

Searches, clicks, device IDs and browsing patterns can reveal behaviour.

04

Context matters

A school logo, uniform or location can identify someone without a name.

05

Metadata

Photos and documents may contain hidden location, author or time details.

06

Purpose limitation

Use information only for the clear purpose explained to the person.

07

Data minimisation

Collect the smallest amount of information needed to achieve the goal.

08

Informed consent

Consent should be clear, voluntary and based on enough information.

09

Audience control

Choose named viewers or a limited audience instead of public-by-default sharing.

10

Retention

Decide how long information is needed and delete it safely afterwards.

11

Strong passwords

Use long, unique passwords rather than reusing one across accounts.

12

Multi-factor security

A second sign-in factor can block access even if a password is stolen.

13

Phishing signals

Urgency, unexpected links and password requests deserve independent checking.

14

App permissions

Review whether an app really needs access to your camera, microphone or contacts.

15

Public networks

Avoid sensitive transactions on untrusted Wi-Fi and use secure connections.

16

Approved AI tools

Check whether an AI service is approved before using confidential information.

17

AI minimisation

Use fictional examples or placeholders instead of real personal details.

18

Human review

Verify AI outputs for errors, bias, invented facts and exposed information.

19

Incident response

Contain mistakes quickly, report them and do not forward exposed information.

20

Digital dignity

Protect other people’s images, messages, stories and choices as carefully as your own.
